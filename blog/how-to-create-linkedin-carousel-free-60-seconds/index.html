How to Create a LinkedIn Carousel in 60 Seconds (Free, No Signup)

By CarouselForge Team · June 16, 2026 · Free Guide

LinkedIn carousels get 3x more reach than regular posts. But most people skip them because they think you need Canva, design skills, or hours of work.

You don't. Here's how to make a professional LinkedIn carousel in under 60 seconds — completely free.

What You Need

  • Your content (a tip list, a lesson, a story — anything)
  • 60 seconds
  • CarouselForge's free LinkedIn Carousel Generator (free, no account required)

That's it. No Canva. No Photoshop. No design skills. If you want to make sure your slides perform well, read our guide on how to create a viral LinkedIn carousel.

Step-by-Step: LinkedIn Carousel in 60 Seconds

Step 1 — Write or paste your content (10 seconds)

Open CarouselForge and paste your content into the text box. It works with:

  • Numbered lists (1. First tip, 2. Second tip...)
  • Bullet points
  • Paragraphs — the tool splits them automatically
  • Twitter/X threads copied and pasted directly

Example input:

5 things I wish I knew about LinkedIn before growing to 10K followers

1. Post at 8am on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday
2. The first 60 minutes after posting is critical — reply to every comment
3. Carousels outperform text posts by 3x on average
4. Consistency beats perfection — 3 posts/week beats 1 perfect post/month
5. Your niche doesn't need to be narrow — your voice is the niche

Step 2 — Click Generate (5 seconds)

Hit Generate Slides. The tool instantly:

  • Splits your text into individual slides
  • Adds a cover slide with your title
  • Formats the typography automatically

You'll see a live preview on the right side.

Step 3 — Pick a theme (10 seconds)

Choose from 11 premium themes. For LinkedIn, these work best:

  • Midnight — dark, professional, high contrast
  • Charcoal — clean, corporate feel
  • Ocean — calm blue tones, great for thought leadership
  • Minimal — white background, very clean

Step 4 — Add your handle (5 seconds)

Type your LinkedIn handle (e.g. @yourname) in the Author field. It appears on every slide — essential for brand recognition when people screenshot and reshare your carousel.

Step 5 — Download (5 seconds)

Click Download All (ZIP). You get high-quality PNG files, one per slide.

Total time: ~35 seconds. The remaining 25 seconds are for admiring your work.

Posting Your Carousel on LinkedIn

This is where most people get it wrong. A LinkedIn carousel is uploaded as a PDF, not as individual images.

Here's how:

  1. Go to LinkedIn → Create a post
  2. Click the document icon (📄) — NOT the image icon
  3. Upload your ZIP file... wait, you need to combine the PNGs into a PDF first

Free way to combine PNGs to PDF:

  • Go to ilovepdf.com/jpg_to_pdf
  • Upload all your PNG slides in order
  • Download the PDF
  • Upload to LinkedIn as a document post

Done. Your carousel is live.

Writing a Caption That Gets Engagement

The carousel is just the visual. The caption drives the algorithm.

Use this proven structure:

Line 1 (Hook): Bold claim or surprising stat

"Most LinkedIn creators are leaving 80% of their reach on the table."

Lines 2-3: Brief context or teaser

"I spent 6 months testing what actually works. Here's what the data shows."

Call to Action:

"Save this post — you'll want to refer back to it."

Asking readers to save your post is one of the strongest signals you can send to LinkedIn's algorithm.

What Makes a Great LinkedIn Carousel

The top-performing carousels all share these traits:

1. Strong cover slide Your cover slide is your headline. Make it specific and promise clear value.

  • ❌ "LinkedIn Tips"
  • ✅ "7 LinkedIn Mistakes Killing Your Reach (And How to Fix Them)"

2. One idea per slide Each slide should communicate exactly one point. If you find yourself writing more than 3 sentences per slide, split it.

3. Big, readable fonts Most people view LinkedIn on mobile. Use a font size that's readable on a small screen without zooming in.

4. Consistent branding Pick one theme and stick to it. Over time, people will recognize your carousels before they even read the title.

5. A strong last slide Your final slide should always include:

  • Your name / handle
  • A clear CTA ("Follow for more" / "Save for later" / "Comment your thoughts")

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Too many slides: 7–10 slides is the sweet spot. More than 15 slides and engagement drops significantly.

No cover slide hook: Your cover is the only slide people see before swiping. If it's boring, they won't swipe.

Missing your handle: People screenshot and reshare carousels all the time. If your name isn't on every slide, you lose the credit.

Uploading as images instead of PDF: LinkedIn treats document uploads differently from image posts. The carousel format (with the swipe gesture) only works as a PDF.
